# KamaAgni writing guide (research-based)

Use this for every new card, task, story or prompt. Each rule comes from a published finding.

| Rule | Why (research) | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Make it doable now.** One clear action, a body part or object, and a time or count. | Ability beats motivation: a behaviour happens when it's easy enough at the moment of the prompt (Fogg, B = MAP). | ❌ "Be romantic." ✅ "Kiss the back of their neck for 30 seconds." |
| **Give the first words.** Starting is the hardest part, so supply an opening line or script. | Planning *when and how* to start roughly doubles follow-through (Gollwitzer & Sheeran meta-analysis, 94 studies, d = 0.65). | Role-play cards now open with a line: "Five digits. We can do this the easy way, or my way." |
| **Build in steps.** Tease, then build, then peak. Don't jump to the end. | Many people feel desire *after* things start (responsive desire), not before (Nagoski; dual control model). | Three beats per role-play. |
| **Clear the brakes, add accelerators.** Mention context: light, sound, privacy, no phones. | Arousal is a balance of "accelerator" and "brake"; stress and interruptions press the brake (Janssen & Bancroft dual control model). | "Set the scene" checklist. |
| **Novelty over repetition.** Prefer things couples haven't done. | Doing new, exciting things together predicts higher desire and more sex on the same day (Muise et al., 2019, J Pers Soc Psychol). | "Not tried yet" shelf; untried-first suggestions. |
| **Talk about it, gently.** Include a question or a debrief, and start soft: feelings and wishes, not complaints. | Sexual communication correlates with better desire, arousal and orgasm across 48 studies (Mallory et al., 2019). Conversations end the way they start (Gottman, "soft start-up"). | "One thing I loved / one thing to try next time." |
| **Make the partner feel seen.** Ask about *their* preferences, say what you noticed. | Partner responsiveness increases sexual desire, especially women's (Birnbaum et al., 2016). Being motivated to meet a partner's needs sustains desire (Muise, sexual communal strength). | "Say one thing they're wearing that you love." |
| **Consent inside the fun.** Every intense card names the safe word or a check-in; every scene ends with aftercare. | The kink community's negotiate → safe word → aftercare model is the standard for safe intense play. | Green / yellow / red; "Colour?" check-ins. |
| **Recognition, not recall.** Show options; never make people remember a feature exists. | Nielsen's usability heuristic #6. | "What next?" panel on every page. |

**Style:** second person ("you"), present tense, plain words, no random Capitals, one idea per card, end with punctuation, at most about 40 words. Explicit is fine; vague is not.

**Never:** anything that sexualises childlike presentation or minors; real non-consent (pretend scenes are always agreed in advance); medical claims without a source.
